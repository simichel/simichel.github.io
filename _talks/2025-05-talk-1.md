Abstract
=========

Accurate simulation of high-velocity impacts between elasto-plastic bodies in Lagrangian coordinates requires robust contact detection algorithms capable of handling the complexity of multiple evolving reference frames. Contact detection is particularly challenging, as each body evolves independently and must coincide precisely when contact occurs.

Traditional methods in explicit hydrocodes employ a two-step process: global search algorithms (e.g., bounding boxes) identify potential contacts, while local search algorithms, like Node-To-Segment (NTS), determine exact locations. However, NTS and similar *a posteriori* local search approaches detect and correct contact only after penetration, neglecting available kinematic data and limiting accuracy in fast dynamic scenarios.

Building upon the foundational work of Heinstein et al., this work presents a novel multi-dimensional local search contact detection algorithm based on the Continuous Extension of Thales' Theorem (CETT). Unlike conventional approaches, CETT performs *a priori* local contact detection by incorporating body dynamics directly into the geometric detection process. It computes contact time and location in one-, two-, and three-dimensional problems through straightforward geometric equations.

We validate CETT through benchmark tests, demonstrating convergence of contact time and location. The method is compatible with both staggered and cell-centered finite volume schemes and is well-suited for parallel implementation with minimal code modification.

**Keywords:** contact detection, Lagrangian hydrodynamics, explicit finite volumes, computational geometry, impact dynamics, *a priori* algorithms.
